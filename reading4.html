Reflection #4

The article from The New York Times named "Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It" talks about an important problem: even though nuclear weapons are very dangerous, most people don't think about the ma lot. People just know how serious nuclear war would be is it starts, but they still don't treat it like a real issue, and instead they focus on everyday life, climate changes and diseases. And this look pretty realistic because people often focus only on things that surround and affect them directly.

I see that the main Idea of the article is that nuclear war feels like it's far away and hard to imagine. It's not something we see or experience, so it's really easy to forget about. I think I can relate to this because I usually worry about that affect me directly, like my homework or what my future will be. Nuclear wear feels like it can happen, but it will not happen soon. So, there is a questioning of my way of thinking: just because something feels war away doesn't mean it's not important.

I also found the idea of something called "moral ambivalence" about nuclear weapons quite interesting. People think that they are dangerous, but at the same time they are needed for protection. I thought at the start that it was confusing, like how it could be protective, but it made sense later. Countries and people want to feel safe by keeping nuclear weapons as a way to scare others who have them too. I think it's really risky, and depends on everyone acting rationally every time, it doesn't really seems reliable to me.

Now, design. It was REALLY surprising. The New York Times made it interactive, with different comic visuals and sections of reading appearing as you scroll the site. It catches attention, and honestly I enjoyed reading this a lot. If it was just a plain text, I would have lost my interest way faster. Instead, I felt more involved, like I was step by step going through all the material, it made it feel real and important rather than something distant and far away.

The article shows that different people see nuclear risk differently. I think it is true because even between people I know, some care a lot about global issues, and others don't think about them at all (I think I'm more like the second ones). Age and experience probably play a big role in this. For example, people who lived during a really tense political times might take it and understand it more seriously.

Overall, this article made me realize that it is the problem that how people easily ignore nuclear weapons and it is not related to them. I also realized that I am a part of this problem since I don't really think about it either. So, the question is: how we can stay aware of something that feels and looks so war away but very serious at the same time.
