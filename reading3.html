Reflection #3

The article named "Motherhood in crisis" made a really strong impression on me because of the way it was presented to people. Instead of being a normal article with just paragraphs of text, it felt more like a visual story, it was a comic book. The way the authors mixed images, short text sections and real life stories made it easer, at least for me, to follow and more emotional to read, to understand. If it was just a plain text, I would have lost my interest way faster. Instead, I felt more involved, like I was step by step going through all the material, it made it feel real and important rather than something distant and far away.

What stood out to me the most was that how each mother's story was broken into different pages with visuals. It really was a graphic novel, where each section and episode shows some story and action. The images and layout helped to show emotions like stress or exhaustion without needing to tell more explanations. And probably because of that I understood the feeling behind the stories a lot more clearly. It looked really personal, it helped to understand what people had to go through to be happy, to be healthy, to be people.

Also I think the format slowed me down in understanding the story, and that is a good thing. Since I had to scroll through each section, I paid more attention to each story instead of rushing though them. They probably wanted to give each mother's experience its own space, and they did, it felt like different chapters of some sort of visual diary.

The stories were powerful. They showed how mothers were trying to balance work, childcare and stress from all of that at the same time. Some of them looked completely overwhelmed, and thanks to the format of a visual novel, it was really visible. The format and the article overall showed it was happening to real people in very intensive ways. I think if it was written as only a text, it would not have felt as emotional or clear. The visuals helped explain the topic and it was easier to understand. It didn't need long explanations, they literally showed and presented these long explanations to our face. It was more relatable and easier to connect with.

Overall, this article and its interactive graphic novel parts made me think differently about how stories can be told. Sometimes, text isn't enough for the article. The comic style helped me understand the emotional side of a motherhood during a bad conditions and crisis mush more that just reading facts would have. It really made me realize how design and presentation can change how strongly we relate and feel about certain topic.
